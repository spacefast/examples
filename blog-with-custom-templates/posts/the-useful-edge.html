The useful edge

Where a product stops is part of its design.

A useful tool makes a promise, then draws a clean line around it. The line keeps the central path legible. It also tells people when they should reach for something else.

Weak boundaries appear late. They let a person invest time and trust, then reveal that the last step belongs to another product, another plan, or a support ticket. Strong boundaries are visible before the work begins.

Scope is not an apology. It is how a product keeps its point of view.

A good handoff names what is leaving the system, preserves the user's work, and gives the next tool enough context to continue. That is not feature avoidance. It is interface design at the edge.

The result is usually smaller software with a clearer promise—and a user who knows exactly when to trust it.
